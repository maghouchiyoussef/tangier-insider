**Short answer: yes, Tangier is generally safe for visitors who use normal city common sense.** Most problems tourists meet are annoyances, such as pushy unofficial guides or overpriced taxis, rather than danger. Here is what to really expect.

## The most common problems (and how to avoid them)

### Do you need a guide?
Honestly, no. Whether you come alone or in a group, Tangier is easy to explore on your own. Every day I see tourists walking around the medina and the city without a guide, often alone or in pairs.

People may still offer to "show you the way" at the port or in the medina, then ask for money. If you don't want a guide, say **"la, shukran"** (no, thank you) and keep walking. If you do want one, book an official guide through your hotel.

### Getting lost in the medina
The old medina is a maze of narrow streets, and some of them really are dead ends. Download an offline map before you go and keep an eye on where you are. If you get lost, head back towards a main street or ask a shopkeeper rather than someone who approaches you.

### Scams
Like in any tourist city, small scams exist, so stay attentive wherever you go. The good news: the police in Tangier are visible and protective of tourists. Living here, I've seen them step in quickly when someone bothers a visitor.

### Taxis
- **Petit taxi** (small blue cars, inside the city): when you get in, ask the driver to switch on the **meter** ("le compteur").
- **Grand taxi** (bigger cars, for longer trips such as **Asilah** or **Chefchaouen**): there is no meter, so ask **how much** and confirm **where you're going** before you get in.

### Pickpockets
As in any busy city, keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket in crowded places like the Grand Socco and the medina's main streets.

## At night

The main streets and the seafront are lively in the evening. At night, simply stay on the main streets where there are people around, rather than empty alleys in the medina, and take a taxi if you are unsure.

From what I see living here, it is rare for a tourist to run into a serious problem such as a robbery or an assault. Use the same common sense as in any big city and you'll most likely be fine.

## Women travelers

Women walk around Tangier normally, alone or together, and it is generally safe. The main thing to watch out for is people approaching you to ask for money: a firm "la, shukran" and walking on is usually enough.

## Useful numbers

- **Police: 19**
- **Ambulance and fire: 15**

## Bottom line

Tangier is a real, busy port city, not a resort. Stay polite but firm, keep an eye on your things, and you will most likely remember the views and the food, not the hassle.
