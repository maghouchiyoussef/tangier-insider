**Short answer: yes, Tangier is generally safe for visitors who use normal city common sense.** Most problems tourists meet are annoyances, such as pushy unofficial guides or overpriced taxis, rather than danger. Here is what to really expect.

## The most common problems (and how to avoid them)

### Unofficial guides
People may offer to "show you the way" at the port or in the medina, then ask for money. If you don't want a guide, say **"la, shukran"** (no, thank you) and keep walking. If you do want one, book an official guide through your hotel. [Youssef: add your own advice]

### "The road is closed"
A classic: someone tells you the street ahead is closed and offers to take you another way, usually towards a shop. Check your map and carry on.

### Taxi prices
Petit taxis should use the meter. Ask before you get in, or agree on the price first. [VERIFY typical fares]

### Pickpockets
As in any busy city, keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket in crowded places like the Grand Socco and the medina's main streets.

## At night

The main streets and the seafront are lively in the evening. Late at night, prefer well-lit main streets over empty alleys in the medina, and take a taxi if you are unsure. [Youssef: add specific advice you are confident about]

## Bottom line

Tangier is a real, busy port city, not a resort. Stay polite but firm, keep an eye on your things, and you will most likely remember the views and the food, not the hassle.
