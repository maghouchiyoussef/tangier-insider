Darija is the Arabic spoken every day in Morocco. It is quite different from the Arabic taught in schools, and it mixes in words from French and Spanish, especially in the north. You don't need much: a few words go a long way, and people in Tangier really appreciate the effort.

## Greetings and basics

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| Hello | Salam / Salam alaykum | sa-LAM a-LAY-kum |
| Thank you | Shukran | SHOO-kran |
| Please | Afak | a-FAK |
| Yes / No | Iyeh / La | EE-yeh / la |
| OK | Wakha | WA-kha |
| Excuse me / sorry | Smeh li | SMEH-lee |
| Goodbye | Bslama | b-sla-MA |
| Good / great | Mzyan | m-ZYAN |

</div>

## Saying prices in dirhams

These are the amounts you'll hear most in shops, cafés and taxis.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Amount | Darija | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| 2 dirhams | Zouj drahem | zoozh dra-HEM |
| 3 dirhams | Tlata drahem | TLA-ta dra-HEM |
| 5 dirhams | Khamsa drahem | KHAM-sa dra-HEM |
| 10 dirhams | Ashra drahem | ASH-ra dra-HEM |
| 11 dirhams | Hdash l-derhem | HDASH l-DER-hem |
| 12 dirhams | Tnash l-derhem | TNASH l-DER-hem |
| 20 dirhams | Ashrin derhem | ash-REEN DER-hem |
| 50 dirhams | Khamsin derhem | kham-SEEN DER-hem |

</div>

<div class="note">
💡 Notice the small change: up to 10 people say <strong>drahem</strong> (dirhams). From 11 to 19 it becomes <strong>l-derhem</strong> (11 is <em>hdash l-derhem</em>, 12 is <em>tnash l-derhem</em>, and so on), and from 20 it is simply <strong>derhem</strong>. Moroccans often write the "a" sound in <em>ashra</em> with the number 3 (3achra) when texting, so don't be surprised if you see it.
</div>

<div class="note">
💡 <strong>Francs, not dirhams?</strong> Among themselves, people in Tangier often count in <strong>frank</strong> (francs): <strong>100 frank = 1 dirham</strong>, so "500 frank" means 5 dirhams. With tourists, sellers usually switch to dirhams. If a price sounds surprisingly high, just ask whether it's in dirhams.
</div>
