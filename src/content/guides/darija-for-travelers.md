Darija is the Arabic spoken every day in Morocco. It is quite different from the Arabic taught in schools, and it mixes in words from French and Spanish, especially in the north. You don't need much: a few words go a long way, and people in Tangier really appreciate the effort.

## Greetings and basics

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| Hello | Salam / Salam alaykum | sa-LAM a-LAY-kum |
| Thank you | Shukran | SHOO-kran |
| Please | Afak | a-FAK |
| Yes / No | Iyeh / La | EE-yeh / la |
| OK | Wakha | WA-kha |
| Excuse me / sorry | Smeh li | SMEH-lee |
| Goodbye | Bslama | b-sla-MA |
| Good / great | Mzyan | m-ZYAN |

</div>

## Saying no politely

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija |
|---|---|
| No, thank you | La, shukran |
| I don't need anything | Ma bghit walou |
| I don't understand | Ma fhemtsh |

</div>

## Shopping and prices

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija |
|---|---|
| How much? | Bshhal? |
| Too expensive | Ghali bzaf |
| Lower it a little | Nqes shwiya |
| A lot / very | Bzaf |

</div>

## Getting around

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija |
|---|---|
| Where is…? | Fin kayn…? |
| Stop here, please | Wqef hna, afak |
| The port | L-mersa |
| The medina | L-medina |

</div>

## In a café

<div class="table-scroll">

| English | Darija |
|---|---|
| Mint tea | Atay |
| Coffee | Qahwa |
| The bill, please | L-hsab, afak |

</div>

<div class="note">
💡 In Tangier many people also speak Spanish and French. Starting with "Salam" and then switching language is perfectly normal.
</div>
