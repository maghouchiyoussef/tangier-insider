Crossing from Spain to Tangier is one of the easiest ways to reach Morocco: the Tarifa ferry takes about an hour and lands in Tangier's city port, right below the old town. Here is how it works in practice, from someone who lives at the other end of the crossing.

## Two routes, two different ports

This is the most common mistake visitors make: the two main crossings arrive at **two different ports**, far apart.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Tarifa → Tangier Ville | Algeciras → Tanger Med |
|---|---|---|
| Arrives at | Tangier **city port**, next to the medina | **Tanger Med**, an industrial port 35–54 km from the city |
| Crossing time | About **1 hour** | About **1.5 hours** (AML lists 2 hours) |
| Companies | **Baleària**, **AML** | **Baleària**, **AML**, **DFDS**, Trasmediterránea |
| Best for | Foot passengers, day trips, first visits | Drivers heading further into Morocco |

</div>

**Visiting Tangier itself? Take the Tarifa ferry.** You walk off the boat in the city. From Tanger Med you still need a taxi, bus or shuttle to reach Tangier: the port's official website gives 20–30 minutes by road.

<div class="note">
ℹ️ DFDS (formerly FRS) announced on its website that it is stopping its Tarifa–Tangier Ville service. Check its site before planning around it.
</div>

## Passport control happens on board

Moroccan police officers travel on the ferry. As soon as the boat leaves, look for the queue and get your passport checked and stamped during the crossing. [Baleària's help centre](https://helpcenter.balearia.com/hc/es/articles/360008110558-Documentaci%C3%B3n-necesaria-para-viajar-a-Marruecos) says you hand your entry document "to the Moroccan police staff you will find on board", and the [official Tanger Med passenger website](https://www.tangermed-passagers.com/fr/mon-voyage/arrivee-a-tanger-med) states that passports are stamped on board during the crossing.

**Don't wait until the end:** the queue gets long, and you can't leave the ship until it's done.

You will need a passport valid for your stay. Check whether you need a visa on your own government's travel advice page.

## Mind the time difference

Spain is usually **1 or 2 hours ahead** of Morocco, depending on the season (Spain uses summer time). Departure times are always in the **local time of the port you leave from**. Check the time zone on your ticket and give yourself a margin.

## From Tangier Ville port into town

- **On foot**: the city port sits right between the old medina and the new town. Both are **less than 15 minutes' walk** from the port exit, the medina slightly uphill.
- **Petit taxi** (the small blue cars): handy with heavy luggage. A ride to the city centre or the medina costs **about 15 dirhams** (local price, November 2026). Ask the driver to use the meter, or agree on the price before you get in.

Expect people offering to guide you as you leave the port. A polite "la, shukran" (no, thank you) and walking on with purpose is usually enough. See [Is Tangier safe?](/is-tangier-safe/) for more.

## Money

The Moroccan dirham can't easily be bought outside Morocco. Plan to withdraw cash from an ATM once you arrive: the new town, a few minutes' walk from the port, has plenty of banks. Many small shops and taxis only take cash.

## Day trip from Tarifa: a simple plan

1. Take a morning ferry from Tarifa and get your passport stamped on board.
2. Walk up to the medina and the Kasbah.
3. Lunch in the old town.
4. Afternoon at Café Hafa, or a taxi to Cap Spartel.
5. Be back at the port at least 1 hour before your return ferry (2 hours with AML).

More ideas: [things to do in Tangier](/things-to-do-in-tangier/).

## Arriving at Tanger Med instead?

Tanger Med is outside the city. According to the port's official website, you can continue by taxi, the ALSA city bus, or a shuttle, and the drive to Tangier takes about 20–30 minutes (35–54 km depending on which part of the city you're going to). Prices are not published there, so agree on any taxi fare before you set off.
